# 01 — Art Direction

## The concept in one sentence

> **Timeless authority meets modern capability.** The paintings say "this is trustworthy, cultured and enduring", and the modern object in the painting says "and it works today".

The style borrows the cultural weight of museum art (Renaissance, Baroque, neoclassical and 19th-century academic painting, Greco-Roman sculpture and architecture) and uses it as a premium brand language for technology. It works because tech brands usually look interchangeable (gradients, 3D blobs, isometric illustrations), while an oil painting of a robed figure typing on a laptop is instantly memorable.

## Why it converts

- **Pattern interrupt:** it looks nothing like the category, so people stop scrolling.
- **Borrowed trust:** classical art suggests permanence, craft and seriousness, which helps money, AI and B2B sell.
- **Literal metaphors make abstract products concrete:** "evaluation" is hard to picture, but golden scales are easy.
- **Quiet UI makes the product look simple and premium.**

## Choosing the variant

| Question | Warm / Aureon | Cool / Contra |
|---|---|---|
| What does the product touch? | Money, assets, property, physical goods | Data, models, code, creativity, research |
| Desired emotion | Security, wealth, stability, heritage | Intelligence, curiosity, taste, the frontier |
| Audience | Finance teams, consumers with money, luxury buyers | Builders, researchers, designers, AI labs |
| Dominant art | Ruins, columns, gold coins, marble, warm landscapes | Portraits, crowds, scholars, armour, instruments |
| Background | Warm paper `#F2F1EE`, white cards | Off-white `#FBFAF6`, teal/mint sections |
| UI dark tone | True near-black `#111214` | Warm charcoal `#2B2B29` |

If unsure, ask the user one question: *"Should it feel like old money (warm) or a modern research lab (cool)?"*

### Hybrid rules

You may borrow **techniques** across variants (e.g. halftone on a warm site), but **never palettes**. Pick one palette for the whole site.

---

## Mood keywords

**Warm:** sun-bleached, marble, ochre, terracotta, gilded, ruins at golden hour, Mediterranean, heritage, vault, keystone.

**Cool:** museum-at-night, verdigris, patina, teal velvet, scholar's study, gallery wall, academy, laboratory, salon.

---

## Art-period palette

| Period / source | Typical use |
|---|---|
| Greek & Roman sculpture, ruins and temples | Foundation, structure, permanence (warm) |
| Italian Renaissance portraits (1450–1550) | Hero figures, faces in profile, laurel crowns |
| Baroque (Caravaggio-style chiaroscuro) | Drama, focus, single-subject cards |
| Dutch Golden Age still life | Craft, abundance, objects, "tools of the trade" |
| Neoclassical / 19th-century academic painting | Clean draped figures, white gowns, crowds in togas (both variants) |
| Pompeii frescoes | Texture backgrounds, warm section breaks |
| Classical landscapes (Claude Lorrain-style) | Calm card backgrounds behind floating UI |

## Composition principles

1. **Subject placement.** The figure usually sits on the right or bottom of the frame and the headline on the top-left. Leave a 40–55% calm area for text.
2. **Cropping.** Crop tight and with confidence: a hand, half a face, a helmet visor. Close crops feel editorial; whole scenes feel like stock.
3. **Edge bleed.** Let hands, drapery or columns enter from outside the frame or cross into the next section. This creates depth and "collage" energy.
4. **Scale contrast.** Pair a huge serif headline with a small figure, or a huge painting with a tiny label. Avoid medium-on-medium.
5. **One focal point per image.** The modern object should be at or near the focal point (the laptop on the lap, the coin between the fingers).
6. **Direction of gaze and gesture** should point toward the headline or CTA.

## Do / Don't summary

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Make one strong image per section | Fill sections with collages of 6 small images (except in a scroll story) |
| Use calm, dignified figures | Use memes, jokes, sunglasses on statues or vaporwave |
| Use crisp modern UI or halftone tech | Paint the laptop in oil style so it disappears |
| Use the same grade across all images | Mix sepia, teal and full-colour images freely |
| Use real whitespace | Fill every gap with texture |
